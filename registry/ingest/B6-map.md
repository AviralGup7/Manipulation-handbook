## What this source is

A clinical psychologist's field guide to covert aggression, written from both
sides of the couch: Simon treated victims and manipulators. Where B3 gives
strategy, B4 the laboratory trigger, B5 the live protocol, and B1 the street
tactics, B6 supplies the **character lens and the clinical tactic catalogue**:
what kind of person runs these plays habitually, what the plays are called,
and what the target's own traits (naivete, over-conscientiousness, guilt-proneness)
have to do with landing them. Its claims are clinical observation, not
experiment; *mechanism* questions still defer to B4 where they overlap, and
*strategic* claims to B3.

## What was cut as padding

* Case-story narration (Joe and Lisa, Don and Al, Mary Jane, James and Jean).
  The tactic survives with one clause of context; the story does not.
* Repetition of the framework at each chapter's opening (the book re-states
  the covert-aggression premise per chapter; stated once, cited after).
* The epilogue's cultural argument (permissive society, media violence). It is
  the author's public-policy case, not mechanism, and does not transfer.
* Anger-management and recovery-industry critique beyond the sentence needed
  to ground each claim.
