\chapter*{How to Read an Entry}
\hbtopicmark{HOW TO READ AN ENTRY}

The handbook is organised by \emph{subject}, not by book. Sources are merged
into a single argument, so a reader never has to know which book said what in
order to follow the text. Provenance is available on demand, not in the way.

\section*{The anatomy of an entry}

Every entry answers seven questions, in the same order, and no two
sections answer the same question.

\begingroup\small
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Core} -- \emph{What is the phenomenon?} The whole entry in
  one to three sentences. If you read nothing else, read this. It is
  boxed because it must never be buried.
\item \textbf{Mechanism} -- \emph{Why does it work?} The psychological or
  social process that causes the effect. A tactic whose mechanism you
  understand can be adapted; one you merely memorise breaks on first
  contact.
\item \textbf{Conditions} -- \emph{When is it likely to work?} When it
  becomes weak or ineffective, and the variables that change the outcome.
\item \textbf{Application} -- \emph{How does it appear in an actual
  interaction?} Observable behaviour and interaction structure, not
  theatrical scripts. Numbered and imperative. This is the slot that
  makes the entry useful defensively: you cannot recognise a sequence
  you have never seen described.
\item \textbf{Feedback} -- \emph{Is it working?} The observable changes
  that say the interaction is succeeding, failing, or producing
  resistance. Bulleted, deliberately behavioural rather than intuitive.
\item \textbf{Failure modes} -- \emph{How does it fail?} What makes
  people suspicious, what creates backlash, what makes the target
  disengage. Every technique in this book has a failure mode, and an
  entry without one is incomplete rather than optimistic.
\item \textbf{Countermeasures} -- \emph{How is it recognized and
  resisted?} Given equal weight to Application.
\end{itemize}
\par\endgroup

\section*{Notation}

\begingroup\small
\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{[B1]}, \texttt{[B3]} -- superscript tags naming the source a
  claim came from. Full details in the companion Source Dossiers volume and \texttt{registry/books.yaml}.
\item \textbf{Drawn from:} -- the entry's complete source list.
\item \textbf{Related:} -- neighbouring entries, named in full. The book is
  a lattice, not a line; follow the links when an entry assumes one. A
  link marked \emph{planned} points at an entry reserved in the registry
  but not yet written.
\item \texttt{T-\,nn-\,nn} -- entry number: chapter, then position in
  chapter. It is printed beside every heading so citations and the source
  registry stay precise; you never need it to read.
\end{itemize}
\par\endgroup

\section*{Order of reading}

Parts~I and~II are prerequisites: they cover what manipulation is, who does
it, and how to read a person. Parts~III--V are the technique layers, and can
be read in any order. Part~VI is defence and can be read first by anyone who
is currently being worked on. Part~VII is the cost accounting.
