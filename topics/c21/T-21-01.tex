\topic{T-21-01}{Total Defeat}{Half-crushed enemies rebuild}

\begin{core}
If a conflict must be fought, the law is bleak arithmetic: crush the opponent's capacity and will completely, or do not start. The half-won fight is the most expensive outcome there is --- the defeated party loses property, standing and face, and remembers all three. Recovery is their life's project, and they have more time than you have vigilance.
\end{core}
\begin{mechanism}
Crushing removes both engines of revenge: capability (resources, allies, position) and legitimacy (the story they tell about the fight). Defeat short of that leaves both running --- humiliated, the opponent reframes the loss as a round, and loss framed as a round recruits. The mechanism is personal: status injuries are processed like physical injuries, and nothing else in human affairs is pursued with the patience of revenge, because the avenger pays no opportunity cost --- rebuilding you is their meaning. Hence the demand for ruthlessness at the moment of decision, when ruthlessness is cheapest: mercy priced as generosity at victory is usually just the purchase of a future enemy at a discount. The merciful alternative exists and is real --- total reconciliation, where the defeated become genuine partners --- but it is a full design with its own costs, not the absence of finishing.
\end{mechanism}
\begin{conditions}
Applies where the conflict is existential and the opponent has proven permanence --- a rival for the same throne, a litigant who will always be adjacent. It does not apply to ordinary friction: colleagues, neighbours, one-time disputes. In reusable relationships the law inverts --- every fight is followed by living together, and the only winning moves are settlements both parties can survive. Misapplying this law to small conflicts is how organisations become prisons.
\end{conditions}
\begin{application}
  \item Before engaging, choose the endpoint in writing: total defeat, total reconciliation, or no fight.
  \item If total defeat: disable capability first --- the resources, alliances and platforms that make revenge possible.
  \item Then address the story: the defeat must be undeniable, or the defeated will simply dispute the arithmetic.
  \item If reconciliation: it must be total --- real standing, real stake, real amnesty --- anything less is the half-crush in softer clothes.
  \item If neither is affordable, do not start. This law's first move is the decision not to fight it.
\end{application}
\begin{feedback}
  \item Working: silence from the defeated side --- actual silence, not the pause between rounds.
  \item Working: their former allies transfer, without being hunted. The system has re-priced you.
  \item Failing: quiet rebuilding --- new allies, reframed history, courtesy that is warmer than the loss justified.
  \item Failing: your own side relaxing into exploitation of the defeated. Nothing recruits revenge faster.
\end{feedback}
\begin{failure}
The law's costs are the mirror of its logic. Total defeat is total exposure: the Crusher becomes the template every observer uses to price you, and allies start planning their exits from your side too. It also forecloses information --- destroyed enemies stop talking, and their knowledge dies unharvested. And history's verdict is consistent: the practitioners who made it a habit end under their own method, because the law has no loyalty clause; it is a tool that survives its owner.
\end{failure}
\begin{countermeasures}
  \item If you have lost, refuse the round framing. Grieve it as final and rebuild a life, not a case --- the round framing is the revenge engine's ignition.
  \item When facing the crusher-type, never fight near your own throne. Make the stakes small and the distance long.
  \item Watch for generosity from a stronger rival mid-conflict: mercy offered before capability is removed is the half-crush in formal dress.
  \item In your own victories, finish the reconciliation or the defeat --- and know which one you chose (T-21-02).
\end{countermeasures}
