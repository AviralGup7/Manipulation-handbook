\topic{T-12-01}{The Patron Map}{Favouritism is the terrain --- map it before it maps you}

\begin{core}
Advancement runs on favouritism more than on merit, and the favouritism has a structure: specific people control specific access, and everyone is sorted into their camp whether they choose one or not. The move is to accept that reality early and map it --- who opens which door, who is rising, whose star is falling --- then attach to the strongest rising patron rather than performing fairness for a meritocracy that does not exist.
\end{core}
\begin{mechanism}
Asked what mattered most to their success, top young executives answered not hard work or performance but \emph{getting in good with the boss}. Favouritism is not a distortion of the hierarchy; it is how the hierarchy transmits reward, and refusing to play does not opt you out --- it just means the benefit flows to rivals who do. The discipline is attachment with concentration: one patron, served intensely, because a person divided across many camps is owned by none. The arrow image makes the point --- one arrow, one target; thought and arrow must become one to pierce anything. Attachment is also insurance: a patron's protection is purchased with the patron's rising fortunes, which is why the map must be re-drawn as power moves, and why the shrewd attach to the mother of the king while she still decides things.
\end{mechanism}
\begin{conditions}
Strongest in any hierarchy with scarce rewards and discretionary gatekeepers --- firms, ministries, professions, institutions. Weakens where rewards are rule-bound and audited (civil-service scales, licensed trades) and in genuinely ownerless markets. The map also ages: patron power is a season, not a mountain.
\end{conditions}
\begin{application}
  \item Draw the map on paper: who controls hiring, budget, visibility, punishment. Gates, not titles.
  \item Mark the trajectories. Who is rising, and whose protection is quietly being withdrawn?
  \item Choose one patron worth serving --- rising, generous to their own, secure enough to defend you.
  \item Serve with concentration: their priorities, their credit, their calendar. Make yourself their answer to a problem.
  \item Re-draw quarterly. Favouritism moves; last season's map is this season's trap.
\end{application}
\begin{feedback}
  \item Landing: work you do for the patron travels with their name on it, and your access rises with theirs.
  \item Landing: other players start treating you as the patron's person --- accurate intelligence.
  \item Failing: your patron stops carrying your work upward. The attachment is being wound down.
  \item Failing: you are spread across three camps, useful to all, protected by none.
\end{feedback}
\begin{failure}
The patron's fall is your fall --- attachment is leverage with a mortar tied to it, and the only defence is early exit, which loyalty makes feel like treason. Concentration also blinds: a single patron's view of the world becomes yours, and you inherit their enemies without their armour. And the whole practice corrodes judgement if it replaces work entirely; favour opens the door, but a patron cannot do the job through you.
\end{failure}
\begin{countermeasures}
  \item If you are the gatekeeper, audit your favourites: are they the best, or just the nearest? Favouritism priced as merit corrupts your own information.
  \item Notice who is uncannily attached to you. Flattery with a calendar is a patron map being drawn around your gates.
  \item Keep one honest channel to power outside your patron --- a single un-bought relationship is a parachute.
  \item When a camp recruits you, ask what the camp costs and who pays. Nothing in a hierarchy is free; some things are only deferred.
\end{countermeasures}
