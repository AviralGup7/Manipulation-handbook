\topic{T-13-04}{The Cutout}{Dirty work done by other hands}

\begin{core}
Power depends on appearances, and the appearance that must be protected is never having done the ugly thing. The move is distance by design: an intermediary does the act, takes the blame, and is himself discarded --- the sacrifice that both cleans your hands and focus the crowd's outrage on someone else's.
\end{core}
\begin{mechanism}
People look outward for causes, not inward: given a visible culprit, the crowd punishes him and the account is closed. The practitioner arranges the culprit. The canonical case is the lord who, having used a hired killer to impose order, then executed the killer with theatrical ceremony --- the population saw justice done, the methods were disowned, and the principal emerged cleaner than before the campaign. A cutout is the same design in organisational cloth: the decision travels through deputies, consultants, "the process", so that the record shows hands other than yours at the moment of harm. The sacrifice is not waste but payment --- the cutout is bought with his ruin, and the theatre of his fall is what converts your liability into your credit.
\end{mechanism}
\begin{conditions}
Strongest where audiences judge by appearances and accountability is theatrical --- politics, courts, large firms, public offices. Weakest where forensics reach intent: audits, discovery, small teams where everyone knows who decided. Also weak against audiences that remember who hired whom; a cutout only works on the audience that did not watch the hiring.
\end{conditions}
\begin{application}
  \item Before an unpopular move, decide who will be seen to have done it. Design, then act.
  \item Route authority through a person with real but deniable discretion --- their signature, your instruction, nothing in writing.
  \item Keep the sacrifice ready: the cutout's exposure must be plausible without touching you.
  \item Stage the accounting. Blame accepted publicly, once, loudly, closes the account.
  \item Never let the cutout know the whole design; a tool that understands the plan can auction it.
\end{application}
\begin{feedback}
  \item Working: outrage attaches to the intermediary's name, not yours, and fades without finding its way upstream.
  \item Working: your standing rises on the correction --- the crowd reads the sacrifice as your integrity.
  \item Failing: the intermediary keeps records, or hints at their insurance. The cutout has become a principal.
  \item Failing: the audience asks the hiring question. Once asked, the design is the story.
\end{feedback}
\begin{failure}
The design trains the organisation: everyone who watches learns that orders are deniable and loyalty is fuel, so the next cutout is harder to hire and the honest staff leave first. It also concentrates risk at the seam --- the one person who knows both your instruction and the act. And the appearance it protects is fragile by definition: hands kept clean by ritual sacrifice stay clean only until an audience that tracks hirings shares a room with one that does not.
\end{failure}
\begin{countermeasures}
  \item Refuse to be anyone's cutout: no discretion without record, no "just handle it" without a paper trail.
  \item When a scapegoating begins, go public early and completely. The design depends on your silence and your isolation; both die in daylight.
  \item Watch the ceremonial sacrifice in your own institutions --- ask who benefits from the fall, and who did the hiring.
  \item Track instruction flows, not just acts. The act points at the cutout; the instruction points at the designer.
\end{countermeasures}
