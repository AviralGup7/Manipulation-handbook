\topic{T-16-02}{Doing Their Thinking}{Supply the conclusion, not the argument}

\begin{core}
Most minds are not argued into new positions; they are walked into them along a path whose steps were chosen by someone else. The practitioner selects which minds are movable, which moments are open, and then feeds structure --- the framing, the order, the two acceptable options --- so the target performs the reasoning and delivers the conclusion as their own.
\end{core}
\begin{mechanism}
The source's chapter premise: the real key to moving people is knowing which minds are movable --- and when silence beats argument. Minds are terrain, like a forest to drive a truck through --- know which trees bend and which will total the truck. The movable mind is one in the yes-no state: ambivalent, recently surprised by consequences, or socially unanchored. Immobile minds --- fixed by identity, incentive or fatigue --- cannot be argued with at any length, and trying spends your credibility funding their certainty. On movable terrain the lever is structural: supply the comparison set, the default, the visible costs of the alternatives --- and let the target do the last step themselves, because a conclusion a person generates is defended like property (T-14-08 is this entry's question form).
\end{mechanism}
\begin{conditions}
Strongest on the ambivalent --- people mid-reconsideration --- and on new arrivals to a topic who have not yet built defences. Weakens on the committed (identity-attached positions harden under argument), on the incentivised (a person paid to disagree is not confused), and under time pressure, which reverts everyone to rehearsed positions.
\end{conditions}
\begin{application}
  \item Classify before talking: movable now, movable later, immovable. Only the first gets your effort.
  \item Find the ambivalence and amplify it --- their own hesitations, stated back to them, do the persuading.
  \item Build the choice so both live options serve you; make the third option invisible, not forbidden.
  \item Feed material in their vocabulary, from sources they already trust. Foreign frames trigger defence.
  \item Stop before the conclusion. Silence plus a well-built path delivers it in their voice.
\end{application}
\begin{feedback}
  \item Landing: they restate your structure as their discovery, with examples you never gave.
  \item Landing: they begin arguing your missing points to third parties --- the conclusion is self-propagating.
  \item Failing: they parrot without conviction. The structure was accepted; the belief was not installed.
  \item Failing: they notice the path --- \emph{why are these the only two options?} --- and the design is exposed.
\end{feedback}
\begin{failure}
Miscalibration is the cost: spend argument on an immovable mind and you have paid to strengthen it. Structured thinking also produces shallow compliance --- remove the scaffolding and the conclusion often falls, because the person never carried the weight. And the craft's ethical edge is sharp: designing the choice set while the target performs the thinking is manipulation by definition, whatever the conclusions turn out to serve.
\end{failure}
\begin{countermeasures}
  \item Notice when your options arrived pre-paired. Ask who benefits from the pairing before choosing between them.
  \item Slow down at your own moments of sudden clarity --- trace where the frame came from before adopting the conclusion.
  \item Refuse imported vocabularies mid-decision; insist on stating the question in your own words first.
  \item Test an installed conclusion by re-deriving it cold. If it cannot be rebuilt from your own premises, it is not yours (T-24-02).
\end{countermeasures}
