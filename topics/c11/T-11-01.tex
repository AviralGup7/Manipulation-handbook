\topic{T-11-01}{The Dependence Build}{Need engineered into a structure}

\begin{core}
Control that rests on affection or argument evaporates; control that rests on the target's needs does not. The build is deliberate: become the sole or cheapest supplier of something the counterpart cannot do without --- reward, income, status, relief --- and let the structure pull. Need, once engineered, is more durable than any pitch.
\end{core}
\begin{mechanism}
Two wheels drive it. The first is the reward schedule: constant generosity trains indifference, because a reward delivered every time is the new baseline. Reward delivered unpredictably produces compulsive persistence --- the source's suitor re-created exactly the pattern trainers rely on, keeping a woman working hard for a five-minute kiss she could never count on. The second wheel is the dependence asymmetry: \emph{I don't need you; you need me}. Whoever can walk away cheapest sets the terms; the practitioner arranges to hold the exits --- a job, an income stream, the only warm relationship, the fix --- while quietly shrinking the counterpart's. B3's illustration is the astrologer who told a kill-happy king he would die three days before him: the king, suddenly dependent on the prediction, spared and enriched the man for life. Dependence, not charm, bought the pardon.
\end{mechanism}
\begin{conditions}
Strongest where the supplied thing is genuinely hard to replace --- a scarce skill, a monopoly, an emotional supply nobody else provides --- and in long relationships where structure has time to set. Weakens fast under substitutes: a second supplier destroys the whole build, which is why the operator polices the counterpart's outside contacts. Also brittle against a counterpart who prices long-term freedom over short-term comfort.
\end{conditions}
\begin{application}
  \item Identify the one supply the counterpart values most and can least easily get elsewhere.
  \item Become its reliable source --- visibly, generously, at first without condition.
  \item After reliance sets in, make the reward schedule irregular. Unpredictability is the leash.
  \item Quietly raise switching costs: intertwine finances, routines, obligations, introductions.
  \item Keep your own alternatives warm and invisible. The sentence running the whole machine is \emph{I could leave}; never let it become false.
\end{application}
\begin{feedback}
  \item Landing: the counterpart plans around your moods rather than around their own goals.
  \item Landing: they tolerate treatment they would have refused in week one --- the baseline has moved.
  \item Failing: they cultivate a second source, and mention it casually. The structure is being unbolted.
  \item Failing: your own need shows --- you need their approval more than they need your supply. The asymmetry has flipped.
\end{feedback}
\begin{failure}
The dependence is mutual the moment the operator starts needing the counterpart's dependence --- needing to be needed is its own need, and the astrologer's bargain runs both ways: the king needed him alive, and he needed the king's belief. Dependent counterparts also degrade as company; a person managed by schedule stops supplying honest signal, so the operator ends up steering a dumbed map. And the moral cost compounds: a structure built to disable another person's exits has no honest name.
\end{failure}
\begin{countermeasures}
  \item Audit your suppliers: for each thing you cannot do without, ask who else provides it. One name on the list is the alarm.
  \item Notice the schedule. Kindness that arrives at random, sized at random, is training you --- name it and the compulsion weakens.
  \item Keep one relationship, one skill and one account fully outside the other person's reach, and defend them.
  \item Price your baseline: what did you tolerate this year that you would not have tolerated before? The drift is the tell (T-14-04).
\end{countermeasures}
