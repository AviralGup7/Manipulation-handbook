\topic{T-11-03}{The Extensive Web}{Two architectures of being needed --- one skill, or many threads}

\begin{core}
Dependence can be built wide as well as deep. The deep build is one irreplaceable skill; the wide build is entanglement in so many threads of an operation that removing you means untangling everything. B3's pair --- Michelangelo and Kissinger --- names the two architectures, and the law's hard rule sits underneath both: never teach them enough to do without you, and never let your need be the visible one.
\end{core}
\begin{mechanism}
The intensive build concentrates: a talent so singular the patron cannot replace it --- Michelangelo could find another patron; the pope could not find another Michelangelo. The extensive build spreads: Kissinger survived Nixon's bloodlettings not by being the best negotiator available but by entrenching himself in so many areas of the administration that removing him meant chaos --- each entanglement a card in his hand and an ally gained. The web variant scales to ordinary careers: the person who runs the onboarding, the vendor relationships, the undocumented process --- no single thread irreplaceable, the knot as a whole uncuttable. B3's chain case adds the passive form: the studio kept the blacklisted writer because the star depended on him --- you can be indispensable in someone else's chain and inherit the protection. The law's corollary is a teaching policy: dependence erodes exactly as fast as you transfer competence, so the practitioner controls the syllabus --- and the corollary's corollary: the thirsted-for is turned his back on; the well keeps its power by not being drunk dry.
\end{mechanism}
\begin{conditions}
The deep build suits scarce-skill markets and creative work; it is hostage to the skill's market (one new technology re-prices a lifetime of intensive investment). The web build suits institutions with many undocumented interdependencies --- firms, ministries, families --- and decays when the organisation documents itself. Both builds fail to the same counter: the deliberate successor. An institution that trains your replacement dissolves either architecture; the defence is owning the teaching rate.
\end{conditions}
\begin{application}
  \item Choose the architecture consciously. Deep if you have (or can bank) a genuine monopoly; wide if you have reach and patience.
  \item For the web: volunteer for the seams --- the handoffs between teams, where nobody else wants to stand.
  \item Keep the load-bearing knowledge out of the documentation: relationships, judgement calls, the why behind the what.
  \item Set your teaching rate deliberately. Train a successor only when your next arch is already built.
  \item Audit the chain: whose indispensability are you riding, and what happens to it if they leave?
\end{application}
\begin{feedback}
  \item Working: your absence is survivable for a day but expensive for a month. The web, not the cog.
  \item Working: reorganisations route around you last. The threads hold.
  \item Failing: a handover document appears with your name on the process. The knot is being published.
  \item Failing: your monopoly skill gets a commodity substitute and the floor drops. The deep build's market risk, realised.
\end{feedback}
\begin{failure}
The web's cost is mobility: extensive entanglement makes you the institution's hostage as much as its ornament --- the person too entangled to leave is owned from the other side. The teaching policy curdles into sabotage when practised on people who trusted the mentoring, and that betrayal, discovered, ends the protector's protection. And both builds share the astrologer's risk (T-11-01): dependence priced in fear invites the first safe moment of retaliation.
\end{failure}
\begin{countermeasures}
  \item Map your own webs: who is load-bearing in your operation undocumented? Cross-train on purpose --- or accept that you are choosing dependence, not efficiency.
  \item When someone is conspicuously indispensable, ask what happens when they are sick for a month. Then ask who benefits from the answer.
  \item Audit mentors and vendors for the teaching rate: what are they deliberately not teaching you? The syllabus is the confession (T-11-01).
  \item If you hold a monopoly skill, watch its market, not your craft. The deep build dies of substitutes, never of critics.
\end{countermeasures}
