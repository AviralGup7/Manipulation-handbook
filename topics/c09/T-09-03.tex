\topic{T-09-03}{The Inherited Shadow}{Succeeding a great name caps you at second place unless you break the course}

\begin{core}
Take over a great name's position and the great name becomes your measuring stick: what came first always reads as the original, and you as the copy. The inheritance opens doors and closes the ceiling at the same time. The escape is not fidelity to the inheritance but a visible break from it --- a new course, a new base, or better, a vacuum where there is no standard but you.
\end{core}
\begin{mechanism}
Judgment anchors on the first-seen standard. A famous predecessor sets the reference price for everything done in their seat, so the successor must accomplish double to appear half; every achievement is booked against what the predecessor would have done. The inheritance is still real capital --- introductions, trust, machinery --- which is why the seat is worth taking and the trap still closes. Breaking the course works by re-anchoring: the comparison is not won, it is made irrelevant, by denigrating the frame and moving in a direction the predecessor never set. The strongest form avoids the comparison entirely: seek the vacuum, the hollowed-out position where there is no competing star, because order restored from chaos is priced against the chaos --- a standard you set yourself.
\end{mechanism}
\begin{tells}
  \item A position where every achievement is phrased as continuation --- carrying someone else's work forward.
  \item Introductions that lean on pedigree: the name opens the door, not the person.
  \item A successor doing everything right by the predecessor's book and quietly underperforming anyway.
  \item Institutions where the founder out-performs every incumbent. The seat is priced, not the person.
\end{tells}
\begin{moves}
  \item Take the inheritance as capital, not as a course. Co-opt what works; break visibly from what defines the predecessor.
  \item Change direction early, while the excuse of novelty is available. A late break reads as failure.
  \item Where choice exists, prefer the vacuum to the contested succession, provided you can actually supply the order.
  \item If you cannot break, change the game: compete in a field the predecessor never played, where the anchor has no readings.
  \item The shadow can also be ridden deliberately --- appropriate the name to gain position, then manage expectations downward before the comparison prices you. Know which phase you are in.
\end{moves}
\begin{counters}
  \item If you hold the great name: choose the successor who will break course, and protect them while they do it. Enforced fidelity is the quieter failure.
  \item Evaluate successors on the course they set, not the continuity they preserve.
  \item Beware vacuum pitches. Chaos is an opportunity only for whoever can supply order; audit the order, not the ambition.
  \item When someone trades wholly on an inherited legend, expect the discard and ask what remains of them when the name is spent (T-22-02).
\end{counters}
\begin{cost}
The break wastes real capital: the name is introductions and trust, and denigrating it reads as ingratitude to the constituency that came with the seat. The vacuum play assumes you can govern what you found; order that fails to arrive is chaos with your name on it. And the pivot is timing-bound --- early it is vision, late it is confession.
\end{cost}
\begin{field}
Successions of every kind: firms, titles, families, schools of thought. Weight highest where the predecessor is living and watchful, because there the anchor is actively maintained.
\end{field}
