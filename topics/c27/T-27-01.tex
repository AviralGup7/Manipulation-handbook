\topic{T-27-01}{The Bill for the Method}{What the operator pays, whether or not they admit it}

\begin{core}
The source closes with an accounting taken from the operator's side of the table: those who run the ruthless course pay the one price that cannot be repurchased --- company. Using people purchases the opposite of what it promises. An operator who will not take morals into the calculation is asked to take arithmetic instead.
\end{core}
\begin{mechanism}
The author learned manipulation from con artists, and watched the part of their lives they never intended to display: not one of the ruthless operators he knew had a real friend; charm won women who wised up and left within weeks; every marriage among them ended in divorce; his prediction for each was a funeral with one attendee. Hardy drank, he adds, not for pleasure. The mechanism is not mystical. The tactics that move people are felt as being moved --- targets register the instrument even when they cannot name it, and they withdraw from it. Meanwhile the operator, rehearsed on strangers, runs the same instruments on everyone in range, including the people a household needs. The old proverb about the harvest survives because it is a good forecast: the crop follows the planting with mechanical indifference to who did the planting.
\end{mechanism}
\begin{conditions}
The bill arrives on a compounding schedule: invisible in the first year, default by the fifth. It is largest for operators who live inside the same social system they extract from --- offices, families, industries with memory. It is smallest, relatively, for the operator who genuinely exits each system before the meter reads --- and that exit is its own bill, paid in rootlessness.
\end{conditions}
\begin{application}
  \item Recognise the bills: countable friendships, intact marriages, the mood of your own house. Each is a line item.
  \item Run the ledger honestly: if the method is worth it, the numbers should survive being written down.
  \item Define worth-it before the tools touch anyone you will see again. After is too late to set the price.
  \item Keep the methods off the people you live with --- not as a courtesy to them, as bookkeeping for you.
  \item When you catch yourself narrating some ruined operator as an exception, stop: the arithmetic has already failed.
\end{application}
\begin{feedback}
  \item Working (as audit): the tools stay sharp for the market and unused at home; the two ledgers stay separate.
  \item Working: people close to you still bring you unpriced information --- the signal that they do not feel handled.
  \item Failing: your household starts talking to you like clients. The instruments have come indoors.
  \item Failing: you find yourself buying company. That is the bill, paid in the only currency it comes in.
\end{feedback}
\begin{failure}
The accounting does not redeem the conduct; it prices it, and prices can be resented as insufficient. A ledger kept dishonestly --- by a man narrating his own loneliness as strategy --- is just the third course wearing a calculator. And some operators genuinely never see the bill, which proves nothing about the bill; the source's gallery also never expected it.
\end{failure}
\begin{countermeasures}
  \item Read the operators around you for the bill's signs: staff churn, exiled friends, the joke about dying alone that lands flat.
  \item Refuse the exception licence. Every ruthless operator in the source's gallery planned to be the exception.
  \item Keep one relationship outside your utility calculus entirely --- off the books, unpriced, undefended. It is the audit trail for your own arithmetic.
  \item If you are inside someone's extraction pattern, remember the exit is real: the pattern cannot price what refuses to trade with it.
\end{countermeasures}
