\case{C-03}{The Neighbourhood Favour}{A year of kindness, priced after the fact --- and the policy that beats the ledger}

\begin{story}
Dennis moves in next to Margaret in March and by April he is carrying her bins in from the kerb, unasked. He posts her parcels, waves off thanks -- \emph{you'd do the same}. In June a lift to the airport; in September two hundred pounds \emph{till Friday}, repaid in October with a plant. The asks thicken: a spare key, \emph{in case of the boiler}; parking for a partner's van; a signature on something he is too busy to read himself. Each request arrives wrapped in the previous favours, and each refusal she can imagine arrives wrapped in guilt. When she finally hesitates over the key, Dennis is wounded --- after all he has done, he had thought they were friends; he would never ask, but the situation leaves him no choice. He does not shout. He goes quiet, and the bins sit out, and her own sense of meanness does the rest of the work. She hands over the key. In January a pipe leaks while she is away; Dennis \emph{lets himself in} to help, and the repair bill arrives beside an unsolicited invoice for his time. Only then, re-reading the year, does she total the ledger the favours were written in.
\end{story}

\begin{lattice}
  \casemove{Contracts signed on her behalf}{T-24-02} The favours were unsolicited by design: each one enrolled her in a commitment she never agreed to, and the enrolment was invisible precisely because the currency --- kindness --- does not look like contracting.
  \casemove{Grief as the collection mechanism}{T-19-05} The wounded silence at refusal was priced to her conscience, not to the loss. The attack's two barrels --- you have hurt me, and you are less good than you think --- both fired, and both were aimed at a woman who hates to think herself ungrateful.
  \casemove{The hurt that was collecting}{T-13-02} The victim posture was aggression in transport. The question was never whether Dennis felt wronged but what the feeling was invoicing: a key, a signature, an open door.
  \casemove{A no that is a policy}{T-24-05} Case-by-case refusal loses to a year of accumulated accounts. The refusal that works is a standing rule --- \emph{I don't hand out keys, to anyone} --- which declines the negotiation rather than the neighbour.
  \casemove{The deed ledger, read late}{T-24-06} Given: bins, parcels, a lift, a plant. Taken: money late, boundaries crossed, a door opened without leave. The record read one direction long before January; it simply had never been added up.
\end{lattice}

\begin{lesson}
The trap was not the favours; it was the unpriced account they accumulated in a currency she was ashamed to audit. Two repairs convert the machine back into a street: name debts as they close --- \emph{dinner Friday and we are square} --- which turns gifts into commerce, and hold one standing no that costs nothing to invoke. The neighbour who cannot survive being squared up with has told you which economy he was running.
\end{lesson}
