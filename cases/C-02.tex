\case{C-02}{The Conference Keynote}{Twenty minutes of visible technique --- and why the room bought anyway}

\begin{story}
Jonas has the slot before lunch: a stage, a wall of screen, a clicker he barely needs. The pitch opens on the number --- \emph{forty thousand data points, the largest study ever run in this sector} --- and the slide behind him is a whitepaper cover with an advisory board of former regulators. The middle of the talk is a vision: the sector transformed, the painful old workflow gone, the early adopters enthroned in the new order. The close is availability: twelve pilot slots, onboarding closes Friday. In question time a sceptic, Dr. Ahn, stands and objects --- the sample, he says, is self-selected and the effect sizes are modest. Jonas does not defend the statistics. He agrees with the concern -- \emph{if I were you I would ask exactly that} --- laughs at himself, finds the common ground (they both watched the 2019 collapse burn buyers), notes that in most cases Ahn's caution would be decisive, and offers him a visible role: chair of the pilot's review board, where his rigour would be the asset. Ahn, half-smiling, sits down. The room reads the exchange as a win. Three pilots sign by Friday. Two are gone by August.
\end{story}

\begin{lattice}
  \casemove{The format answers first}{T-14-01} Stage, screen, cadence and the rehearsed number are features, not content: the room's fast system committed before the method section would have been reached.
  \casemove{Authority as wardrobe}{T-18-01} The whitepaper and the regulator-heavy board are proxies for expertise, produced for a fraction of what expertise costs --- which is exactly what the proxies were doing.
  \casemove{The number that is one team's counting}{T-15-01} Forty thousand data points is a single counting exercise repeated, displayed so the count itself reads as confirmation. No one in the audience checks the independence of the sample; the size stands in for it.
  \casemove{Selling the absence}{T-15-04} The vision slide offered relief from the sector's daily grind --- the fantasy floor --- and it out-competed any accurate description of the rollout, which would have been labour, meetings and doubt.
  \casemove{Friday as a wall}{T-10-01} The closing date manufactured a loss frame around an opportunity that had no fixed supply. Scarcity of the offer stood in for scrutiny of the offer, and the clock capped the week in which anyone might have checked the whitepaper.
  \casemove{The objection, processed}{T-16-04} The question-time performance ran agreement-first: feelings agreed, common ground built, the objection honoured as the exception, face returned to the objector with a title attached. Ahn was managed, not answered --- and he left with standing, which is why he sat down.
\end{lattice}

\begin{lesson}
Nothing here was hidden; the audience knew about deadlines and flattery, and it worked anyway, because the moves arm one another: the deadline compresses the verification window, the number makes the sceptic look like the outlier, and the authority props make checking feel unnecessary. The lattice breaks at its cheapest node --- one honest read of the whitepaper's method section collapses the number, and with it the board, the vision and the Friday. Verification is expensive in exactly the unit the design taxes: time. Spend one unit anyway.
\end{lesson}
