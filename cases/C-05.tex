\case{C-05}{The Practitioner's Bill}{The method works for fifteen years --- the last case is what it costs}

\begin{story}
Adrian is good. A rail logistics coordinator with fifteen years of results, he reads interests before words, prices deeds instead of promises, and has never once lost an exchange he chose to enter. His colleagues learned this the way a coastline learns weather. Requests to him arrive pre-conceded; information arrives late --- routed around him the way water routes around a rock it cannot move. His performance reviews are flawless and his leaving drinks would be sparsely attended, if the thought ever occurred to anyone to hold them. Then comes storm season. The network needs voluntary overtime, and voluntary is the operative word: the goodwill he is asking for is stock he never stocked. Compliance he can still get --- the tools still work --- but the marshals who once traded him favours trade them elsewhere now, and his own house runs the way he runs it: efficient, priced, and quiet. Standing on the platform at midnight, watching other people's teams solve his problem with enthusiasm he has no credit to draw on, Adrian finally names the thing his method never priced: he won every exchange, and lost the pond he was fishing in. Nobody showed him the finish line. He had been optimising a game whose payout changed without announcing it.
\end{story}

\begin{lattice}
  \casemove{The bill arrives whole}{T-27-01} Fifteen years of exchange-winning was a loan taken against relationship capital that was never rebooked. The storm season called in the balance at the exact moment the balance mattered.
  \casemove{Wins that eat the pond}{T-27-02} Each victory was real and each one taxed the reserve the next request would draw on. The win-rate was the problem --- nothing was feeding the water he fished.
  \casemove{The siege economy}{T-27-03} The stockade held; sieges are what it cost. Provisioning a defensible position out of other people's goodwill works until the one season you need the gates open from the outside.
  \casemove{The shape people plan around}{T-28-01} Formlessness was the one method he never used. Fifteen years of consistent winning made him a fixed shape --- predictable, routable, avoidable --- and the network, which is fluid, simply flowed past the rock.
  \casemove{A finish line nobody drew}{T-28-02} He never once asked what the game was paying out. The goal line moved in his absence; the techniques kept scoring in a sport whose prizes had changed.
\end{lattice}

\begin{lesson}
The closing case is the lattice turned mirror: every entry that modelled leverage models its cost, and the operator who never leaves the workshop pays compound on all of them at once. The handbook's tools are real --- Adrian's results were never fake. The lesson is arithmetic, not morality: some assets --- reserve, goodwill, the benefit of the doubt --- can only be stocked by losing exchanges on purpose, and a practitioner who prices everything eventually owns a ledger full of winnings the world will not honour.
\end{lesson}
