\dossier{B8}{T-23-05}{\bookshort{B8}}{PSPR 10(3):214--234; Results (determinants of accuracy)}

\begin{distilled}
  \item The measured ceiling under the instrument: across 206 studies unaided behavioural reads average 53.98\% correct, so the window buys discipline, not certainty --- an effect of $d \approx .40$ is real but far too thin to carry a verdict alone.
  \item The corpus confirms the window's two design choices: hearing beats seeing (audio $d = .419$, audiovisual $d = .438$, video-only $d = .077$), which is the case for the L-squared dual capture; and baseline sampling is the one receiver-side lever that measurably lifts accuracy (55.91\% vs 52.26\%, $d = .239$).
  \item It also confirms the humility clause: professionals equal novices ($d = -.025$), so the method --- not the reader's seasoning --- carries whatever accuracy exists.
\end{distilled}
