\dossier{B4}{T-10-03}{\bookshort{B4}}{ch. 7, pp. 205--210}

\begin{distilled}
  \item Scarcity is delivered as a limited quantity, a closing date, or both, and the two reinforce each other.
  \item A limited quantity imports crowd evidence as well as rarity, since the number signals that others want the thing.
  \item A deadline removes the option to postpone, which forces a decision under the ambiguity in which shortcuts are followed.
  \item Newly appearing scarcity is more powerful than scarcity that has always been present, because the change implies information the target does not have.
\end{distilled}

\begin{unique}
  \item The deadline analysed as a degradation of decision quality rather than as an increase in the object's value.
  \item Novelty as an independent amplifier, and the resulting conclusion that announcing a limit matters more than having one.
\end{unique}
