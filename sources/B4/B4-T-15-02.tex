\dossier{B4}{T-15-02}{\bookshort{B4}}{ch. 4, pp. 126--156}

\begin{distilled}
  \item Conformity to crowd evidence rises sharply where the situation is ambiguous and the individual has no independent basis for judgement.
  \item It rises again where the crowd is composed of people similar to the individual; resemblance is what makes the behaviour relevant.
  \item Urgency compounds ambiguity by removing the time in which it could be resolved.
  \item A single matched witness can outweigh a larger unmatched group.
\end{distilled}

\begin{unique}
  \item The two conditions named as switches rather than as degrees, and the interaction between them.
  \item The consequence for practice: resemblance is manufactured because it is cheaper and more effective than numbers.
\end{unique}
