\dossier{B4}{T-09-01}{\bookshort{B4}}{ch. 4 (Social Proof), pp. 126--156 (additive)}

\begin{distilled}
  \item Reputation functions as accumulated social proof: the stored testimony of what others did, which future observers treat as evidence about the person.
  \item Uncertainty sends evaluators to the crowd's record; a cultivated reputation is that record, curated before the evaluator arrives.
  \item B4's boundary applies to reputation too: the proof of others must be perceived as voluntary and unpurchased, or the mechanism reverses and the crowd's verdict turns.
\end{distilled}
