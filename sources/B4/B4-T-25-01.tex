\dossier{B4}{T-25-01}{\bookshort{B4}}{ch. 3, pp. 87--125}

\begin{distilled}
  \item A decision taken on favourable terms frequently survives the subsequent revelation that the terms were less favourable.
  \item The effect depends on the change arriving after the commitment rather than before it.
  \item Commitment generates its own justifications, and those justifications attach to the decision rather than to the price.
  \item The practice is described in ordinary car sales, where the quoted figure is corrected upward once the paperwork has begun.
\end{distilled}

\begin{unique}
  \item The timing condition: the same revision disclosed before the decision fails, and after it succeeds. This is what distinguishes the technique from ordinary hard selling.
  \item The absorption range growing with the counterpart's own investment.
\end{unique}
