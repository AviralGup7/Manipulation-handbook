\dossier{B4}{T-14-03}{\bookshort{B4}}{ch. 3, pp. 87--125}

\begin{distilled}
  \item A small initial commitment raises the rate of compliance with a much larger related request made later.
  \item The effect persists after the original incentive is withdrawn, because the commitment has generated supporting reasons of its own.
  \item Commitments that imply a self-description are stronger than those that imply only a behaviour.
\end{distilled}

\begin{unique}
  \item The self-generating property: new reasons assemble after the position is taken and outlive the inducement that produced it. Neither B1 nor B3 describes this mechanism.
  \item The distinction between a commitment's cost and its reach, with the reach being what matters.
\end{unique}
