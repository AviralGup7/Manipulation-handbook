\dossier{B4}{T-13-01}{\bookshort{B4}}{ch. 4, pp. 126--156}

\begin{distilled}
  \item Audience reaction can be staged, and a staged reaction moves a real audience in the intended direction.
  \item Agreement is inferred from visible traces, so controlling what is visible controls the inference.
  \item The construction requires only a minority of participants; the rest follow by copying what is displayed.
  \item Applied to recorded reactions, planted audience members, and presentations in which dissent is not visible.
\end{distilled}

\begin{unique}
  \item The economics of the construction: a minority of seeds, no coordination between them, and no persistence required beyond the moment of decision.
  \item The resulting strategic conclusion that the venue matters more than the argument.
\end{unique}
