\dossier{B1}{T-20-01}{\bookshort{B1}}{Tactic 12 (ch. XIII), pp. 153--160}

\begin{distilled}
  \item Pressure --- a single hard choice, flatly delivered --- is admitted as having its place, and the place is narrow and seldom.
  \item Most people cannot be swayed even by masterly persuasion; wisdom is recognising those dead ends in advance so effort is not poured into unwinnable cases.
  \item For the rest, the fell stroke lands when the target is caught between the comfort of agreeing and the fear of refusing.
  \item Its power is cumulative: prior hours of rapport and credible reason are what make one hard moment read as decision rather than attack.
  \item Applied without that context it is plain coercion, and it cannot be repeated --- the first stroke spends the surprise the second would need.
\end{distilled}
