\dossier{B1}{T-27-02}{\bookshort{B1}}{ch. XVI, pp. 179--182}

\begin{distilled}
  \item Selective ruthlessness fails: the operators all planned to be good to chosen friends, and all failed the same way.
  \item The doctrine: a person's behaviour changes very little from one relationship to the next; the man who stabs enemies stabs friends too.
  \item The eagle image gives the mechanism --- a single justified exception locks the pattern; momentum runs one way and each selfish act lowers the cost of the next.
  \item The golden rule is commended as self-interest: the long-haul dividend of conduct you would accept repays better than extraction.
  \item Over-grabbing has a direct market price: grab too much on this deal and the counterpart cuts you out of the next one.
\end{distilled}
