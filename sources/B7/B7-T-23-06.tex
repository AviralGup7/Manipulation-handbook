\dossier{B7}{T-23-06}{\bookshort{B7}}{chs. 5--6 (pp. 35--50)}

\begin{distilled}
  \item The witness oath as taxonomy: 'tell the truth' covers lies of commission (the bald falsehood); 'the whole truth' covers omission (the withheld remainder); 'nothing but the truth' covers lies of influence (statements that manage perception instead of conveying information).
  \item Commission is checkable and expensive; omission is comfortable and transfers the work of assembling the false picture to the listener; influence is the most powerful and least recognised.
  \item The influence strategy's fingerprints (ch. 5): nonanswer statements ('that's a good question', 'glad you asked'); repeating the question to buy time; referral statements ('as I said before'); selective memory ('not that I recall'); exclusion qualifiers ('basically', 'for the most part'); perception qualifiers ('frankly', 'to be perfectly honest'); invoking religion ('I swear to God'); overly specific answers; convincing statements --- biography offered where facts were owed.
  \item Answering a question with a question is NOT automatically deceptive, against other schools (B7 explicit, ch. 5).
  \item Denial problems: an explicit denial of the act itself is often absent from a deceptive answer --- the subject denies around the act, not the act.
\end{distilled}
\begin{unique}
  \item The three-strategy taxonomy read off the oath, the influence-strategy framing, and the catalogue of convincing-statement fingerprints are unique to B7 in this corpus.
  \item B7's explicit exception that answering a question with a question is not automatically deceptive is unique.
\end{unique}
