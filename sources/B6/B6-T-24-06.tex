\dossier{B6}{T-24-06}{\bookshort{B6}}{ch. 10, pp. 122--136}

\begin{distilled}
  \item The most fundamental rule of human engagement: the aggressor sets the rules --- the attacked party is always scrambling for balance, so the first principle is to stop reacting and start pricing.
  \item Judge actions, not intentions: never mind-read why somebody did something hurtful --- there is no way to really know, and in the end it is irrelevant.
  \item Accept no excuses: a person resisting submission to the principle of civil conduct will engage in the behaviour again; dropping the excuse lets the conduct be labelled plainly.
  \item Accept only direct responses: manipulators are notorious for never giving a straight answer to a straight question; re-ask respectfully --- hostility is construed as an attack and licensed as war.
  \item Promises mean nothing until the person demonstrates willingness to interrupt their usual manipulations; only changed behaviour is evidence of change.
  \item Take action quickly: a train without brakes is easiest to stop when it just begins to roll --- aggressive personalities lack internal brakes.
  \item Make reasonable agreements --- appropriate, reliable, verifiable, enforceable --- and stop fighting battles that cannot be won; persisting at unwinnable battles is where the depression comes from.
\end{distilled}
\begin{unique}
  \item The aggressor-sets-the-rules principle and the excuses-as-forecasts rule are unique to B6 in this corpus.
\end{unique}
