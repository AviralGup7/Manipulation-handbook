\dossier{B3}{T-14-05}{\bookshort{B3}}{Law 8, pp. 61--66}

\begin{distilled}
  \item When you force the other person to act, you are the one in control; it is always better to make your opponent come to you, abandoning his own plans in the process.
  \item Lure with fabulous gains --- then attack: you hold the reins, and the moving party arrives already invested.
  \item The law's cases reward the stationary position: terrain, timing and terms are set by whoever does not travel.
\end{distilled}
