\dossier{B3}{T-13-03}{\bookshort{B3}}{Laws 3 and 4, pp. 17--36}

\begin{distilled}
  \item Keep people off balance and in the dark: never reveal the purpose behind your actions, and they cannot prepare a defence.
  \item Our first instinct is to trust appearances --- false sincerity, decoy objects of desire and red herrings throw the target off the scent.
  \item People can focus on one thing at a time: the bland exterior, like the unreadable poker face, hides intention behind the comfortable and familiar.
  \item Bismarck's speech for peace before the war he intended: guide them down the wrong path until it is too late.
  \item Say less than necessary: the more you talk, the more common you appear and the more likely to say something foolish; silences put others on the defensive and they fill them, revealing weaknesses.
  \item Reversal: no smoke screen survives an established reputation for deception.
\end{distilled}
