\dossier{B3}{T-12-02}{\bookshort{B3}}{Laws 20 and 18, pp. 145--152, 130--138}

\begin{distilled}
  \item It is the fool who always rushes to take sides; do not commit to any side or cause but yourself.
  \item Independence makes you the master of others: played against one another, they pursue you; the Virgin Queen gives hope but never satisfaction.
  \item The fortress is the same error in stone: isolation cuts you off from information, makes you conspicuous, and the citadel is betrayed to the first enemy.
  \item Louis XIV at Versailles is the counter-model: place yourself at the centre of things, where everything moving passes you.
  \item Narrow reversal: isolation in small doses, as a deliberate workshop for thought, with the return road kept open.
\end{distilled}
