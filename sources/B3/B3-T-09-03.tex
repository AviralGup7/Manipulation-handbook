\dossier{B3}{T-09-03}{\bookshort{B3}}{Law 41, pp. 369--378}

\begin{distilled}
  \item What happens first always appears better and more original than what comes after; a famous predecessor or parent sets the standard every successor is measured against, and the successor must accomplish double to look half.
  \item The inheritance is real but capped: the name wins a following at the start, and wins the ceiling with it.
  \item The prescribed escape: denigrate the past and its claims, move in a totally new direction, and create your own world rather than compete inside the predecessor's frame.
  \item Where there is a choice, prefer a vacuum of power --- a position with no strong incumbent, where you can bring order out of chaos without competing with another star.
  \item The reversal: the shadow can be ridden as a deliberate trick --- the emperor case used an illustrious grand-uncle's legend to gain office, then visibly broke from its expectations and kept the public from demanding its heights --- and the past holds elements worth appropriating outright, as the conqueror who learned army organisation from his father.
\end{distilled}

\begin{unique}
  \item Re-anchoring as the mechanism of the break: the comparison is escaped not by outdoing the predecessor but by changing the axis of judgment so the comparison has no readings.
  \item The vacuum preference stated as a positioning rule, not an adventure: choose the emptiest competitive field you are capable of ordering, because restored order is priced against the chaos you found.
  \item The ride-and-discard variant with its timing condition: appropriate the name to gain position, then manage expectations downward before the inherited standard prices you --- which requires knowing which phase you are in.
\end{unique}
