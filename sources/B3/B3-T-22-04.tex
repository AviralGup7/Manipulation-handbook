\dossier{B3}{T-22-04}{\bookshort{B3}}{Law 44, pp. 375--390}

\begin{distilled}
  \item The mirror reflects reality but is the perfect tool for deception: mirror your enemies, doing exactly as they do, and they cannot figure out your strategy.
  \item Held to the psyche, the mirror seduces with the illusion of shared values; held to behaviour, it teaches a lesson in the target's own handwriting.
  \item The neutralizing mirror mocks and humiliates, making the enemy overreact; Ivan the Terrible's Tartar enthronement adds caricature so the reflection cannot be missed.
  \item The Shield of Perseus authority: face Medusa through the reflection, never directly.
  \item The source's own warning: beware mirrored situations --- two mirrors facing each other produce escalation, not information.
\end{distilled}
