\dossier{B3}{T-04-01}{\bookshortB3}{Law 33, pp. 281--290}

\begin{distilled}
  \item Everyone has a weakness, described as a gap in the castle wall.
  \item The gap is usually an insecurity, an uncontrollable emotion or need, or a small secret pleasure.
  \item Once found it functions as a thumbscrew that can be turned to advantage.
\end{distilled}

\begin{unique}
  \item The specific enumeration of what the weak point usually is --- insecurity, uncontrollable emotion, need, or small secret pleasure --- rather than a general claim that everyone has one.
  \item The naming of the device as a thumbscrew: incremental pressure applied to a small point rather than force applied to the whole person.
\end{unique}

\begin{terms}
  \item \emph{thumbscrew} --- a small, repeatable pressure applied to a known weak point.
  \item \emph{gap in the castle wall} --- the single undefended place in an otherwise defended person.
\end{terms}
