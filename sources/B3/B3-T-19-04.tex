\dossier{B3}{T-19-04}{\bookshort{B3}}{Law 17, pp. 121--130}

\begin{distilled}
  \item Humans are creatures of habit with an insatiable need to see familiarity in other people's behaviour; your predictability gives them a sense of control.
  \item Turn the tables: be deliberately unpredictable; behaviour that seems to have no consistency or purpose keeps others off balance.
  \item Fischer--Spassky: wild demands, late arrivals, forfeits --- then, precisely when the model said broken, a ferocious prepared game; scrambled patterns did damage no opening theory matched.
  \item Picasso cut off his dealer without explanation and let the man spend days re-modelling; the balance of power returned reset.
  \item Reversal: sometimes a known pattern lulls the opponent to sleep behind their preconceptions --- a smoke screen worked from predictability itself.
\end{distilled}
