\dossier{B3}{T-21-06}{\bookshort{B3}}{Law 39, pp. 325--332}

\begin{distilled}
  \item Anger and emotion are strategically counterproductive; you must stay calm and objective.
  \item If you can make your enemies angry while you stay calm, you gain a decided advantage: the agitated reveal, overcommit, and mistake the trembling for strength.
  \item Find the crack in their vanity through which you can rattle them, and you hold the strings.
  \item The tea master's remark to the samurai: bait aimed at reputation is answered by demonstration or not at all.
  \item Reversal: the game is well known --- some opponents stir you precisely to make you simple; the sophisticated target is unmoved by bait and baiter alike.
\end{distilled}
